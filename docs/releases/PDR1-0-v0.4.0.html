Three admissions

These are first because they outlast the fixes. Two are governance gaps this release created; one is a defect nobody has filed.

Governance · owner: Samuel

Two shipped features have no requirement at all

Access gating and the Socratic teaching protocol are both live in this release, and neither appears in the spec. That means neither can carry a row in the traceability matrix — a row naming a requirement the spec does not define is an orphan, and the CI gate rejects it. They are recorded as owned gaps instead. Inventing requirements to cover work already done would be the matrix laundering the problem rather than reporting it, which is the one thing a computed matrix exists to prevent.

Capability · blocks judging any teaching change

Nothing in the build can tell whether the teaching improved

The Socratic rewrite, the Arabic/English mixing, the tutor drifting to a previous question — every one of them can only be assessed by a human reading a transcript. The evaluation harness asked for in the original feedback does not exist. Until it does, every change to how the tutor teaches ships unverifiable, including the largest change in this release.

Defect · on nobody's list

The tutor assumes every student is a boy

Twenty-three masculine pronouns in the lesson prompts alone, more elsewhere, and a masculine Arabic form offered to the model as a worked example. There is no gender column in the database, so there is nothing to fix it from. A girl using this is described to the model as "he", and the model follows. Found in review, not reported.

The fixes

As before: what was reported, what was actually wrong, what changed. On three of these the reported symptom and the cause were not even in the same layer of the product.

The mastery score that looked broken was not broken

#17 #27FR-1003
He saw
Mastery jumping 30 → 69 → 30 during normal use. Separately, the tutor saying "92%, that's excellent" mid-lesson with nothing to attach the number to.
Actually wrong
Replayed through the real model, that sequence is reproduced exactly, and it is correct. Mastery is a belief — the probability the student knows the skill — and with a 20% guess rate and a 10% slip rate, two answers genuinely move it that far. The defect was that we showed it. The lesson printed mastery 30% → 69% after every single answer, on the student's own screen, ungated. And four separate places put the same raw figure into the tutor's prompt, which is where "92%" came from — it was reading out a line that said mastery today 92%.
Changed
All five now carry the named band. The lesson reads attempted → proficient, or still attempted when the band holds. The prompt blocks carry an explicit instruction never to quote them — copied from the engagement block, which has had one since it was written.
Side effect
The signature animation reserved for reaching "mastered" had been written months ago and never once rendered, because nothing in the product displayed a band change for it to attach to. It now has one.
Left open
The band still moves two steps on two answers, because the belief genuinely does. Withholding a band until there is enough evidence to hold one is a change to what the product claims about a student, so it is a decision, not a fix.

A fourteen-year-old was being offered the extraction pipeline

#10 #11 #12
He saw
Admin tabs — pipeline, content, evidence walk — visible to end users.
Actually wrong
The student's navigation bar read Study · Where you stand · Evidence Walk · Content · Pipeline. A graph explorer, a content review queue and an extraction pipeline, in the same row as her lesson. The front page also led with a card inviting her to walk the pipeline.
Changed
The tabs and the card are gone, and — because unlinked is not hidden — the routes themselves return 404 on the student build. Guessing the URL no longer works.
Not a fix for
This is a build-time switch, not a permission system, and the code says so in both places. It makes the student build not carry the routes; it cannot tell one person from another. Roles need accounts, which are deferred. A login-shaped thing that checks nothing would have been worse than an honest flag.
Caught in test
The first implementation was wrong and looked right. Two of the pages are prerendered, so the gate ran at build time and stayed 404 even with the override on. Only serving the built app both ways revealed it.

The tutor had no way to ask an open question

#33 #34 #35 #24 #23SC-005
He saw
Five separate things: no Socratic method, no open-ended questions, the lesson not ending on retrieval, steps locked in before he was asked to try them, no interest in his working, explanations arriving as one block.
Actually wrong
One cause. Every tool the protocol gave the tutor for making a student do something produces a card — a bank question, a widget, a figure. There was no way to express "ask her, in words, and wait". A model told to make the student act, holding only card-shaped tools, produces a quiz. And the lesson arc ended, literally, with "closing recap message" — so not ending on retrieval was the specification, not a drift from it.
Then found
The Arabic protocol has always had the instruction naming a chat question as a valid ask. Mathematics — the subject of this pilot, the one he tested — never did. The rule was written once and reached one subject of three. That is a worse finding than "we never thought of it".
Changed
Five rules, in the shared rhythm so all three subjects get them: an open question is a first-class ask; never state a step you haven't asked him to try; ask how he got there; work with a partial answer instead of demanding the final one; and end the lesson on retrieval from memory, before any recap.
Cost
The turn cap went from 14 to 18. Asking before explaining costs turns — that is what it is. At 14 a full lesson hit the cap before the retrieval could happen, so this fix would have been silently skipped on exactly the lessons that ran long.
Unverified
This is teaching behaviour and judging it needs a real multi-turn lesson nobody has run. The failure mode to watch for is over-correction — a tutor that interrogates a tired student instead of helping her.

What the AI actually costs, by what spent it

#39FR-C04
He asked for
Cost tracking broken out per function. Filed as blocked, for discussion.
Actually the case
Much smaller than it looked. Every model call has recorded its function, its cost, both token counts, the cache counters, latency, student and environment for weeks. Nothing read any of it — the only query touching that table outside the insert path counted rows for a stat tile. A reporting gap wearing an instrumentation gap's clothes.
Built
A page grouping spend three ways: by function (a taught lesson against a quick revision against a one-off question), teaching against photo uploads, and per student with a projected month — the only basis a price can be built on. Scoped to one environment, because a blended cost figure is a plausible wrong number somebody would price against.
Not verified
It compiles and builds; no query in it has been run, because the session that wrote it has no database. Worth a look on the box before the numbers are trusted.

The report showed her the gaps and offered no way back in

#29
He asked for
A third option after a lesson: review my weak spot.
Actually the case
The report ends by naming exactly what she did not get, then offered two ways out — look at the graph, or leave. Neither did anything about what it had just told her.
Changed
A third door, in four lines, because the mechanism already existed: the practice loop is already built weakest-first. What was missing was a way into it from the one screen where a student has just been told where she is weak.

The tutor could answer the question before last

#22
He saw
The tutor drifting off the current question and explaining the previous one.
Actually wrong
Not a grounding failure — the right material was assembled. The whole question bank sits in the tutor's context and every question already used is still in the transcript, so it has to infer which one is current, and nothing told it how. The irony: the browser already computes this exactly, deterministically, for a different fix in the last release. That value is never sent to the model.
Changed
A rule naming the most recent pushed question as the one under discussion, and telling it to check a result's id before reacting.
If it recurs
Stop making it infer. Send the value the browser already has, so the current question is stated rather than deduced. Held back deliberately — that is plumbing that needs a live session to test, and a prompt rule was the cheaper thing to try first.

A CI gate that had never once run

Found
While updating the requirements for the previous release.
Actually wrong
The script that regenerates the requirement counts searched for a section heading that does not exist — its own error message names the right one, so a rename changed the message and not the code. The counts table had therefore been hand-maintained all along and had drifted five requirements and eight tasks out of date. Worse, the command returned a failure code on every run, and continuous integration runs it as the first line of a fail-fast block: the release would have gone red on arrival for a reason having nothing to do with it.
Changed
One word. The block is generated for the first time, and running it twice is now a no-op — which is what the CI check was always asserting and never able to.

The twenty still open

None of these is unanswered. Each carries what is true today and what it is waiting on. They cluster into four things.

4 issuesblocked
Waiting onAccounts. Signup, admin roles, a landing page, resuming a lesson.
Why it is not a small jobStudent identity today is a database sequence that a routine content reload truncates and restarts, carried in a cookie the code itself documents as "not a security boundary". Saving real lesson history against it would produce history a maintenance operation silently deletes.
2 issuesneed a decision
Waiting onSamuel. Whether the graph explorer stays a student surface at all; how much textbook grounding a student should see.
Why they are stuckBoth are product calls with defensible answers either way, and both would be wrong to guess. The second sits directly on the grounding principle the product is built on.
5 issuesshipped, unproven
Waiting onA real lesson. The whole Socratic cluster is fixed in code and unverified in behaviour.
Why they stay openClosing them would claim a result nobody has seen. They close when someone runs a lesson and reads the transcript — which is also the argument for building the evaluation harness.
9 issuesdesign and scope
Waiting onDesign work, a deploy, or a screenshot. A friendly progress view, a public link, microcopy to point at.
Why they are not blockedNothing technical stands in the way of any of them. They need someone to decide what the thing should look like, which is not a thing to guess at from a ticket.
