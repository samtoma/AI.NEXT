Noor · release v0.11.0 · 2026-10-02

A map that follows the book

Tamer rebuilt the Your Progress page on 1 October. Samuel accepted it on 2 October. The map is now organised by chapter, and the chat beside it is twice as wide and knows what you are looking at.

What changes for a student

Changed The map is organised like the textbook

Before
Every objective was a card in columns: the first column needed nothing earlier, the next built on it, and so on. All the links were drawn at once, with no arrows.
Now
Each chapter is a circle, Term 1 on top and Term 2 below, in book order. Inside, its lessons sit on an inner ring and their objectives on an outer ring. Colours still show how far the student has got.

Changed Zoom in for detail

How
Zoomed out you see the chapters; zoom in to see lesson names, then each objective. Tap something to zoom to it; tap empty space or press Esc to go back. Drag, pinch, scroll and the + / − / Fit buttons all work.

Changed The lines

Arrows
A line with an arrow means "learn this first". It points to the objective that builds on it.
Dashed
The link crosses into another chapter. These are always shown.
Solid
The link stays inside one chapter. These appear when you select something.
Thin grey
Not a prerequisite: it joins a lesson to its own objectives.

Changed Ask Noor is wider and knows your selection

Before
A 300px column beside the map, with no idea what the student was looking at.
Now
Up to 620px wide on a large screen, and still fits beside the map on an iPad in landscape. Select a chapter, lesson or objective and ask about it: Noor answers about that, and says what it builds on and what builds on it.

Fixed The chat no longer jumps after you answer a widget

In v0.10.0, if a widget grew after you answered it (for example, to show feedback), the chat could scroll back to the top of that message. Now the view stays where you are.

Still open

Open Try it on an iPad

Pinch and drag have not been tried by hand, nobody has used the map on a real iPad, and the Social Studies and Arabic maps have not been looked at. Automated tests cover the layout on the real maths book.
