Noor · release v0.12.0 · 2026-10-12

Students now say which curriculum they follow

Samuel asked for two things on 25 September: digest the Siyavula Grade 10 maths book, and let a student choose "American" or "National" when they sign up. This release is the second thing in full, and the machinery for the first. The book's content itself is not in this release.

Read this first

Not included A deploy does not put the Grade 10 course on the site

What a deploy does
Runs seven database changes, builds the new app, and adds six entries to the Prep-3 mistakes catalogue (below). It loads no course and switches nothing on for any student.
How a course gets on the site
A separate, manual step in GitHub: Load a course. You deploy first, then run it as dry-run, rehearse, load. Even after it loads, the course stays hidden until someone switches it on for a grade on the Courses page.
Where the content is
Only in a local pilot database. It holds 65 lessons, 211 objectives and about 5,300 live questions across the 14 chapters. The repository carries 13 of the 14 chapters' bundles (Chapter 8's sits in an untracked working folder), no whole-book export, and the book's config still says "ingest". So Load a course cannot yet load the whole book from this release.
Who has read it
Nobody. Chapter 8's first two checkpoints were decided by Samuel; every other checkpoint was passed automatically by the AI checks. The new Review page is how a person will read it.

What changes for a student

New Sign-up asks which curriculum your school follows

Before
Everyone was stored as "National" without being asked.
Now
After the grade, sign-up asks "Which curriculum does your school follow?" National or American, neither pre-selected, and the account is not created without an answer. If nothing is live for that grade in a curriculum, it is still offered, with the note "Nothing to study here yet for Grade 10".
Why
Samuel, 1 October, after seeing sign-up skip the question for grade 10: "yes the sign up should always ask".

New A first Google sign-in gets one "Welcome" screen

Why
Google tells us a name and an email, not a grade.
Now
The student answers grade and curriculum once, before any lesson. Until they do, every student page sends them back to that screen. A second try is refused, so it can never become a way to change a curriculum.

Same Students who already study see exactly what they saw

Their curriculum
National, recorded as "implied" because they were never asked.
Proof
The database change gives every existing student the "implied" label and alters nothing else. Tests pin what the course gate shows a National student, and CI's migration proof reads an ordinary student back as National, implied.

Heads-up A student who picks American sees an empty home, for now

What they see
"[Name], nothing's ready here yet. This isn't anything you did — the moment a subject is ready, it'll show up on this page by itself." Their header reads "Grade 10 · American" and carries no ministry-syllabus line.
Why
A student sees only the courses of their own curriculum, and no American course is loaded or switched on. This is also what a grade-10 student sees today, picking either curriculum.

Changed Two Prep-3 widgets behave a little differently

Angle setter
It used to open on one fixed position, which was already the correct answer to a "35°" inscribed-angle question, so pressing Check without touching anything counted as right. It now opens somewhere that question's answer is not. Nine Prep-3 widget questions use it.
Line drawer
It said "you swapped the coordinates" even when a point had never been moved. Now it says it only when both points were moved and both are swapped. Four Prep-3 widget questions use it.
Curve sketcher
Unchanged for the linear and quadratic sketches: the old marking is kept as a frozen copy and replayed against every live curve question, and the two must agree.

Changed What the tutor can offer a student who cannot see a subject

Before
The tutor could offer a "switch to another subject" card to a student who had no access to that subject, and she landed on a not-found page.
Now
It offers only subjects the student can open, and the server removes any card that slips through. A student who sees all three Prep-3 subjects gets the same prompt as before. Samuel approved this and two smaller prompt differences: the tutor's list of source books names only books the student can see, and the Arabic lessons' printed names, which only appear once the Arabic course is reloaded.

Fixed Two doors that should have been closed

Lesson script
Typing ?mode=read&lesson=… by hand opened the teaching script of a hidden course. It now goes through the same course gate as everything else.
Figures
Any signed-in student could fetch any figure by its number, including a hidden course's or another curriculum's. A figure is now served only when it belongs to a course that student may see. A refused figure looks exactly like one that does not exist.

What changes for an operator

Changed Courses is organised by curriculum

Layout
Each curriculum has its own section, with grades named in its own words ("Secondary 1" for National, "Grade 10" for American), and a line per grade showing which curricula have something live. The American Grade 10 course is listed, hidden by default.
Beside each switch
How complete the course is: objectives, questions, the book-section checks and the pipeline's coverage audit.
Safety
Before you hide a curriculum's last live course for a grade, the page says how many students that leaves with nothing to study. A number, never a name. Switching on a course with nothing loaded warns: "Students would see an empty course."

New The student record shows and changes the curriculum

Who
Operators with the student-data role only.
Shows
The curriculum, whether it was chosen or implied, a flag for a value the product does not know, the change history with the operator's name, and which courses the student can see and why. An exception for a course outside their curriculum is labelled as exactly that.
Changing it
The page asks first and names which courses the student will stop and start seeing and that progress is kept. Nothing is deleted: change it and change it back, and mastery, attempts and saved places are exactly as they were. The students list gains a curriculum column.

Changed No figure mixes two maths courses

Where
The Overview, Content, /pipeline and /gallery each name the course they count. The Teaching page says which courses Socratic probing can reach (never the Grade 10 course at launch).

New A Review page, aiming at zero

What it is
Every maths item that no human has signed, one at a time: questions and their worked solutions, widget claims, misconceptions, worked examples, objectives, prerequisite links, figure stand-ins, the automatic checkpoint decisions, and any step the working checker flagged. For Samuel, Tamer and Kamil (the content-review role).
Verdicts
Approve (the human stamp), ask for a fix, or reject (the question stops reaching students; it is never deleted). Two reviewers never get the same item, and every verdict is kept with who and when in a record nobody can edit.
Heads-up
"Reviewed" now means a person signed it, nothing else. It covers Prep-3 maths too, so items there that were promoted in bulk or passed only an AI check appear in the backlog from the first day. The size of production's backlog has not been counted.

Changed Loading and refreshing content, on noor

Load a course
New. Adds one course that is not there yet. It backs up and checks the backup first, refuses rather than delete any student data, and can replay one course's reviewed export while keeping every student's progress.
Content refresh
It used to act on the frozen baseline's stack. It now acts on noor. Its "course" mode only adds; "update" edits; "replace" prunes and makes you type the number of affected students. The two modes that could wipe the database or promote held questions are gone from noor.

Watch The Prep-3 mistakes catalogue changes in production

What
The deploy re-syncs the Prep-3 misconception catalogue, as it does on every deploy. This time it adds six entries for the probability lesson on combined events (each with a refutation), gives 26 existing entries an alias list, and re-labels the kind of 19. No entry is removed or renamed. The sync can also re-point the mistake tag on a book question's answer choices, never a question's status.
Proof so far
CI replays the deploy on a real database, twice, with students' attempts citing mistakes, and checks nothing live is lost. That step was green on the merge. The rehearsal against a copy of the actual Prep-3 bank (T424) is the second check.
T424
Passed (12 October). On a copy of the Prep-3 database, the deploy took the catalogue from 97 to 103 entries (only the six new ones), added 7 answer links on five probability questions and changed none, kept all 17 recorded student mistakes resolving, and left every Prep-3 question, status and picture as it was. Running it twice changed nothing, and the rollback worked. Two things to expect after the deploy: the console will show every live book question as not yet reviewed by a person (the old "AI dual-check" stamps move to their own field), and 14 old widget links that can never fire stay in the live data until a later clean-up. Full report: specs/003-curriculum-tracks/rehearsal-t424-2026-10-12.md.

What is built but not switched on

PieceWhat it does once the Grade 10 course is loaded
The course itselfSiyavula Everything Maths, Grade 10, in book order with no school terms. The tutor names "this book" and its pages, speaks English only and uses no Arabic anywhere for an American student. No Socratic probing. Hidden until switched on.
The whole book, visibleThe picker lists every chapter and lesson. A lesson not yet prepared reads "Being prepared" and cannot be opened; the moment a chapter loads, its lessons open. The course card reads "5 of 65 lessons ready".
Parts of a section stay togetherA section the book splits into parts is taught in order, and the student's place stays in it until every part passes. On the Your Progress Map such a section gets a frame. National maps are unchanged to the byte.
Typed maths answersMarked by what they mean: factorised and expanded forms are the same expression; several values in any order; intervals, coordinate pairs, surds. If a form is asked for, the other form is "wrong form". An answer the marker cannot read is asked for again, never marked wrong. No AI marks. No Prep-3 question carries a marker, so Prep-3 is marked as before.
Five new interactive widgetsArea model, box plot, polygon builder, solid scaler, Venn builder. The curve sketcher also learns hyperbola, exponential, sine, cosine and tangent.
The line that produced itObjectives, prerequisite links, generated questions, widget questions and the mistakes catalogue come out of one gated, metered run, documented well enough to ingest the next book. Metered spend for this course: about $953 in all (API-equivalent, a floor), of which the full-book run, about $800, was inside the approved range of $0.85–1.1k.
